    OURS TO CHECK: act-let-scope, let-shadow-scope, scope-let-arm-global,

    COST: batch with item 1.

4.  THEIR_QUESTION  real-atan2 and the negative zero
    PR 107. `real-atan2` branches on `y >= 0.0`, and `-0.0 >= 0.0` is true in
    IEEE, so it answers +pi where zig answers -pi, and +0.0 where zig answers
    -0.0. Our table covers only positive zeroes, so nothing tests it either
    way. They recorded it in the chapter as a semantic choice rather than
    fixing it, and asked: "If you have a view on whether we should match zig
    there, we would take it."
    A view is owed. This is a language question, not a plug question.

5.  OUR_BUG  plugs row 2.02 is open, and the open half is our wrong claim
    PR 103. We wrote that a payload-carrying constructor is never a duplicate.
    It is: `emit-zig-match-arm` emits a BARE prong when the binders are all
    unused (ZigEmitter :2155 one sub, :2158 several), so two such arms on the
    same constructor still collide and still refuse at zig.
    `zig-pat-switch-value` only answers for `list-length subs == 0`, so that
    shape is under-dropped. Their words: "the direction is safe, nothing is
    miscompiled, and the fix is to mirror those two conditions."
    A NEXT PR THAT WRITES ITSELF -- narrow, with a test shape we already know.
    Also recorded, not changed: `zig-arm-shadowed` scans backward per arm and
    allocates a Text per comparison, so emission is O(n^2) in arm count.

6.  NEEDS_INVESTIGATION  scan-char-literal has no unterminated diagnostic
    PR 114, and they found it while verifying ours. A quote followed by a
    newline consumes the newline AS the character, finds no closing quote, and
    emits a CharLiteral without complaint. Same family as COMPILER-35,
    different shape, still open, and nothing in the tree discriminates it.
    We already own the repro shape from PR 114; this is the sibling.

12. NEEDS_INVESTIGATION  SyntaxNodes.codex does not cite what it uses
    It uses `for x in ... -> ...` comprehensions, which desugar to `map-list`,
    and its only cite is `Foreword chapter Maybe`. Nothing in the compiler
    cites `Foreword chapter ListUtils` except `Core/Collections.codex`, which
    nothing cites either. So SyntaxNodes compiles only where a bundle happens
    to add ListUtils directly -- which `bundle_parse.ps1` does, as its first
    chapter, and every other ladder bundle inherited from it.
    MEASURED HERE, not reasoned: adding SyntaxNodes to the lex bundle gave 20
    CDX3002 "Undefined name: map-list", and adding ListUtils cleared all 20.
    SAME SHAPE AS THE copy-sx-diag PROBLEM one line up: a chapter that does not
    declare what it needs. Worth an issue once we are through the queue, and
    worth checking whether the CITE checker can see it -- `build/check-subset-
    cites.ps1` exists and this got past it.

13. WE_OWE_THEM  the U54 lexer's new deck copy is not in our PR 114 account
    `lex-errors-push` is NOT the rename our first read called it. U54's lexer
    rewinds the bivy after every token, so a diagnostic built there would be
    overwritten; the new function copies the cell onto the deck through
    `copy-sx-diag 0` first. Their own prose says three sites pushed cells
    directly before 2026-09-01 and all four lexer refusal tests crashed
    reading them. Our PR 114 report describes a smaller change than landed.
    Nothing is wrong on their side; our account of it is just thin.


14. NEEDS_INVESTIGATION  Syntax/Lexer.codex depends on the deck intrinsic and
    declares nothing. It has NO CITES AT ALL, at U53 or U54. U54's
    `tokenize-collect` rewinds the bivy after every token via `lex-state-below`
    and is correct only if `deck-record` really moves `make-token`'s record --
    which happens only when `deck-record-intrinsic` is on, which the emitter
    gates on `init-phase-allocator` being present among the IR DEFS
    (`X86_64Chapter.codex:1264`). Seven chapters cite `Codex chapter Phase
    Allocator`; the lexer, the one that now needs it, is not among them.
    WHY IT IS SHARP: the gate is defeated by DEAD-CODE ELIMINATION, not just by
    a missing chapter. `opening.codex:1359` runs `ir-prune-unreachable-roots`
    on every CDX compile, so a unit that carries the Phase Allocator chapter
    but never CALLS `init-phase-allocator` loses the def and silently gets the
    identity. A bundle can satisfy every cite, carry the right chapter, and
    still be compiled without the mechanism.
    MEASURED HERE: our desugar rung carried the chapter and page-faulted at
    `tokenize-collect+546` exactly as lex did; adding the driver's prologue
    fixed it and put `init-phase-allocator` back in the symbol map.
    WHY THEY CANNOT SEE IT: their deck-less bundles are plugs, and plugs consume
    IR rather than lexing. Nothing on their side lexes without a deck.
    THIS IS PARTLY OURS -- our harnesses never did the driver's setup -- and the
    half worth sending is that a silent default on a gate whose input can be
    pruned is a miscompile with no diagnostic. A cite on Lexer.codex is the
    cheap half; a loud failure when the lexer's deck requirement is unmet is
    the real one.

15. WE_OWE_THEM  the copy-sx-diag/SyntaxNodes and map-list/ListUtils pair
    Two more chapters that do not declare what they need, both found the same
    morning. `Syntax/Lexer.codex` calls `copy-sx-diag` from SyntaxNodes without
    citing it; `Syntax/SyntaxNodes.codex` uses `for..in..->` comprehensions
    (which desugar to `map-list`) and cites only `Foreword chapter Maybe`.
    Both fail LOUDLY at compile, so they are cheap -- unlike 14. Worth one
    issue together with 14, since the shape is identical and the contrast
    between "fails loudly" and "page-faults at run time" is the argument.
    `build/check-subset-cites.ps1` exists and all three got past it.
